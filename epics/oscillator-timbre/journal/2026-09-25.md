# 2026-09-25: the design, before any code

This entry records why the epic looks the way it does; the implementation entries will follow.

The conversation started from a simple question: could the oscillator morph between other
waveforms, as Sytrus does? The first answer was cautious, a selector for the morph target with
the current sound kept by default, because compatibility of saved sounds seemed to matter. The
owner removed that constraint outright: the presets may move, what matters is the best experience
for the user. That reopened the design, and the first idea was then the simplest one, a single
Shape knob over a longer path, sine to triangle to square to saw. The owner saw its flaw before I
did: a chain only interpolates between neighbours, so it loses the sine-to-square fade that is
the instrument's current sound and never offers sine-to-saw at all. A selector of what sits at
the right end of the knob gives every fade from the sine, and the sine is what a kick starts
from. So the selector came back, for a better reason than compatibility, with one constraint from
the owner: it lives next to the knob it changes, not elsewhere in the panel.

Tilt entered as a comparison. The question was whether phase distortion could replace the
selector; the honest answer was no, it covers different ground: even harmonics, which nothing in
the instrument produces today, and every point of a two-dimensional plane with Shape, but not the
exact triangle or saw spectra. With the goal stated as "about every kick there is", the owner
chose both, and the overlap near the saw was accepted as two roads to neighbouring sounds rather
than as redundancy.

Tribe kicks then tested the scope. Their sound is mostly a distortion chain and what is filtered
after it, and for a moment the epic grew a distortion stage with a character selector and a tone
control. The owner cut that back with a question worth keeping: does the plug-in work for tribe if
distortion is left to other plug-ins? It does, because the source decides what a distortion
produces, and the sweep, the long tonal tail played at the note and the waveform are what an
external chain cannot fabricate. The owner's next point settled the filter: without one, an
external saturation receives everything, so a filter before it shapes the result; and a static
filter would kill the attack along with the tail's fizz, so it must move. The one thing a filter
inside the source can do that the track's chain cannot is follow the pitch, and the pitch sweep
already is the kick's brightness envelope. Hence Tone, in octaves above the instantaneous
frequency, bypassed by default. A high-pass at the note was considered and declined: nothing
lives below the fundamental in this signal, and a steep high-pass near it costs transient
precision for no gain.

Two engineering decisions were taken alone and are marked for confirmation in the requirements.
Band-limited tables reverse a sentence the README states as deliberate; the reversal follows from
the owner's priority (a saw that starts a sweep at 2 kHz aliases badly, and "always a kick" cannot
carry that), but the owner has only agreed to it in passing. Moving Pitch source and MIDI channel
to the top bar follows from needing a twelfth cell in the grid; the owner has not seen that
layout. Both are the kind of thing invariant 5 of the epic skill says to confirm rather than to
stall on. The default sound staying bit-identical is not up for discussion: it is what lets every
new feature be added without a single existing project sounding different at Shape 0.
